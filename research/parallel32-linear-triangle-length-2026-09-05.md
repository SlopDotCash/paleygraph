# A uniform linear bound for one-orbit triangle derivations

**The Paley conjecture and the Proximity Prize remain unproved.**
This note bounds the length of a derivation using one fixed orbit of
zero-sum triangles. It does not bound the number of six-term relations.
The argument is ordinary mathematics, with separate-author review and
Lean verification outstanding. No literature novelty is claimed.

## 1. Statement

Let n be a power of two, H=<g> a subgroup of order n in an odd
characteristic field, and A={1,g^a,g^b} a distinct zero-sum triangle.
Put c=gcd(n,a,b) and m=n/c. Thus A is contained in H_m=<g^c>, and m
is the least order of a subgroup containing its normalized entries.
For such a triangle m>=4. Work in the integer ring

    R_n = Z[X]/(X^(n/2)+1),     f=1+X^a+X^b.

For an opposite-free target multiset S, let F be its reduced formal
coefficient vector and r=||F||_1 its number of entries. Assume that S
has a triangle derivation using scaled copies of this fixed A. Equivalently,
assume q=f^(-1)F has integer coefficients in R_n. Then

    minimum number of copies of A = ||q||_1
      <= r sqrt(3m(m-epsilon)/2) <= (4/3) r m,             (1)

where epsilon=1 if m is 1 modulo 3, and epsilon=-1 if m is 2 modulo 3.
In particular a generated six-term target has minimum length at most
8m, hence at most 8n.

The minimum is relative to this one triangle orbit. If other triangle
orbits exist, allowing them might shorten it further. If the quotient
is not integral, (1) does not assert that the target is generated.
In particular field vanishing alone is not the integrality hypothesis.

For a primitive six-set, every minimum graph for this fixed orbit has
cycle rank L/2-2, as in pass31. Consequently that rank is at most 4m-2.
Moreover, a primitive target generated by this orbit lies entirely in
one multiplicative coset of H_m.

## 2. A reference trinomial with an explicit integral inverse

First take a power of two m>=4, put d=m/2, and use R_m. The reference
polynomial is h=1+X+X^2. Its inverse has coefficients

    d=1 mod 3:  (1,0,-1,1,0,-1,...) truncated to d entries;
    d=2 mod 3:  (0,-1,1,0,-1,1,...) truncated to d entries.       (2)

To check (2), coefficients away from the boundary satisfy
b_j+b_(j-1)+b_(j-2)=0. The two boundary coefficients of hb are
b_0-b_(d-1)-b_(d-2)=1 and b_1+b_0-b_(d-1)=0. These identities also
hold at d=2, where X^2=-1 and h=X.

The nonzero coefficients are all +1 or -1. Their number is

    t=(m-epsilon)/3.

Thus h is a unit of R_m and

    ||h^(-1)||_1 = ||h^(-1)||_2^2 = t.                       (3)

No finite-field estimate or theorem about cyclotomic units is needed:
the displayed coefficient vector proves the inverse identity directly.

## 3. Fourier comparison controls every normalized trinomial

Suppose f=1+X+X^b in R_m. Let u and v be complex numbers of absolute
value one. The reverse triangle inequality gives

    |1+u+v| >= ||1+u|-1|
             = |1+u+u^(-1)|/(|1+u|+1)
             >= |1+u+u^(-1)|/3
             = |1+u+u^2|/3.                               (4)

The equality uses |1+u|^2-1=1+u+u^(-1), which is real, and |u|=1.
For a primitive m-th root zeta and every odd j, set u=zeta^j and
v=u^b. Since h is a unit, h(u) is nonzero; (4) proves that f(u) is
nonzero too. Multiplication by f is therefore invertible over Q.

Evaluation at the d primitive m-th roots is a Fourier transform
on the d reduced coefficients, with orthogonal columns of squared
length d. Parseval, (4), and (3) imply

    ||f^(-1)||_2^2
      = (1/d) sum_(j odd mod m) |f(zeta^j)|^(-2)
      <= (9/d) sum_(j odd mod m) |h(zeta^j)|^(-2)
      = 9t.

Cauchy--Schwarz for the d coefficients now gives

    ||f^(-1)||_1 <= 3 sqrt(dt)
                  = sqrt(3m(m-epsilon)/2)
                  <= 4m/3.                               (5)

For the last inequality, squaring reduces it to
27(m-epsilon)<=32m. When epsilon=1 this is immediate. When epsilon=-1,
a dyadic m>=4 with m=2 mod 3 is at least 8, so 27<=5m.

The same estimate holds for f=1+X^a+X^b whenever at least one of a,b
is odd: swap them if needed, then apply the automorphism X -> X^v
with av=1 mod m. This is a signed permutation of the reduced
coefficient basis, preserving both norms and integrality. It sends
one of the two nonconstant exponents to 1.

## 4. Smaller conductors do not introduce an ambient-order loss

For the original exponents, put Y=X^c. Then f belongs to the embedded
ring R_m, with exponents a/c,b/c, at least one of which is odd. Its
inverse also lies in this smaller ring. Embedding its coefficient vector
in R_n just inserts zeros, so its coefficient norms are unchanged.
This proves (5) with m, not n, for the original f.

Signed cyclic convolution obeys

    ||FG||_1 <= ||F||_1 ||G||_1;

each coefficient product contributes once with a sign, and subsequent
cancellation can only decrease the absolute sum. Taking G=f^(-1)
proves the upper bound in (1).

For completeness, the minimum-length equality does not require an
optimization search. Every L-copy triangle derivation writes q as L
signed monomials. Hence L>=||q||_1. Conversely, the reduced integer
coefficients q_j give exactly |q_j| signed monomial copies at each
position, achieving ||q||_1. The quotient is unique because f is
invertible over Q. This is the argument from
[pass31](parallel31-short-multiples-2026-09-05.md), now combined with
a uniform inverse estimate.

Every copy g^j A lies in a single coset of H_m, and opposite entries
lie in the same coset because -1 belongs to H_m. All cancellations
therefore occur inside cosets. Each nonempty surviving part in a
coset is itself a zero-sum multiset. A primitive target can have only
one such part, proving the last assertion of Section 1.

The integrality restriction has a precise index explanation. Suppose,
additionally, the field is F_p and multiplication by the smaller-ring
trinomial has determinant p. The ring R_n is a free R_m-module with
basis 1,X,...,X^(c-1), so multiplication by f has determinant p^c in
R_n. Evaluation at g has kernel K of index p, whereas fR_n has index
p^c and lies in K. Therefore

    [K : fR_n] = p^(c-1).                                 (6)

When c>1 this is a strict inclusion: some field relations cannot be
generated by that triangle orbit. Equation (6) does not say that one
of those missing relations has six terms. When c=1 it recovers the
index-p equality used in the earlier fixed-field example.

For a primitive six-set the connectivity proof in pass31 applies to
any minimum derivation by a fixed orbit: a nonempty collection of
minimum copies has a nonzero quotient vector, hence nonzero formal
boundary. Multiple components would split the target into proper
zero-sum subsets. With (3L-6)/2 edges and L vertices its cycle rank is
L/2-2. Substitution of L<=8m gives the claimed upper bound.

## 5. Exact checks and the earlier arithmetic example

The [verifier](../experiments/parallel32_verify_2026_09_05.py) checks
all 252 normalized trinomials 1+X+X^b for m=4,8,16,32,64,128 and
0<=b<m. Repeated or canceling exponents are deliberately included
in these algebraic checks. For each polynomial it constructs an exact
rational inverse by the previously checked norm recursion, and checks
the inverse independently by signed cyclic row equations.

It then verifies the two norm inequalities by integer cross-multiplication,
as well as convolution with a six-term signed target. It checks the
explicit inverse (2), five lifts to larger ambient orders, and all
119 earlier minimum-length certificates. The
[result](../results/parallel32_verification_2026_09_05.json) records
2,352 checks and about 1.50 seconds of elapsed time for this run.
The uniform Fourier argument is not inferred from these finite cases.

At p=215535361, n=m=128, f=1+X+X^19, the exact inverse coefficient sum is

    ||f^(-1)||_1 = 2878824541/215535361.

Thus every generated six-term target in that ring has

    L <= floor(6 ||f^(-1)||_1) = 80,

compared with the general upper bound 8m=1024. Here the determinant-p
property from pass30 implies integrality for every field relation.
The complete eligible finite census still has actual maximum 44;
the upper bound does not replace or weaken those exact minima.

No new Lean build or hosted proof job was run. The new verifier and
ordinary proof are by the same author; separate-author review remains open.

## 6. What still prevents the desired counting bound

This closes a specific uncertainty left by pass31: a six-term relation
generated by one triangle orbit never requires more than a linear
number of triangle copies as the dyadic order grows. Large derivations
are controlled in length, although the earlier 38--44 examples show
that a fixed list of very small patterns is insufficient.

It does not follow that the number of six-term outputs is cubic.
For example, merely counting integer quotient vectors of absolute sum
at most 8n in dimension d=n/2 gives the crude upper bound

    2^d binomial(d+8n,d),

by choosing absolute values and then signs. That expression grows
exponentially in n and is far too large. This only describes the loss
in that direct enumeration bound, not an impossibility theorem for
other uses of the inverse estimate.

The missing input must bound the number of quotients whose product
with f has exactly six surviving entries, and must also handle fields
with multiple triangle orbits or relations outside a chosen orbit's
integer ideal. No uniform upper bound on D6, stronger cancellation
exponent, full classical Paley proof, or official prize certificate
is supplied here.
