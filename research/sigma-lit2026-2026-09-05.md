## 1. What the 2024–2026 literature says about the three status questions

### 1.1 Two arbitrary sets (CITED)

Fouvry, Shparlinski, Xi, *Estimates for trilinear and quadrilinear character sums*, arXiv:2404.09295, v4 of 19 Mar 2025, to appear in Rev. Mat. Iberoam. (abstract and HTML v4 fetched). §1.2 states the conjecture for the bilinear form `𝔅(α,β) = Σ_{m∈M} Σ_{n∈N} α_m β_n χ(m+n)`: "It is conjectured, for instance with just constant weights α_m=β_n=1, that 𝔅(α,β)=o(MN) for all subsets M,N with M,N>p^η and η>0" and immediately: "This is far from proven, and the classical inequalities of Vinogradov (1.4) and Karatsuba (1.6) still stand." Their own Theorem 2.1 bounds `Σ_{k≤K} Σ_{m≤M} Σ_{n≤N} α_m β_{k,n} χ(ak+bmn)` with `M` an interval `[1,M]`, `M > 4p^{1/(2r)}`, and arbitrary weights in `(k,n)`; the interval variable is essential (Burgess shifting). Assessment: needs structure (one interval); not two arbitrary sets.

No 2024–2026 item found in any sweep improves Karatsuba's range for two arbitrary sets, and none improves Chang's `4/9` result for sets with small doubling (the workspace's obligation §6.6). The closest structured-side progress: Alsetri–Shao, arXiv:2509.07765 (local copy `sources/alsetri-shao-2509.07765v1.html`), Theorem 1.1: "Let p be prime and let χ (mod p) be a nontrivial Dirichlet character. Let A ⊂ F_p be a proper GAP of rank 2. If |A| ≥ p^{1/4+ε} for any ε>0 then |Σ_{n∈A} χ(n)| ≪_ε p^{−δ}|A| for some positive constant δ=δ(ε)>0", i.e. Burgess quality for rank-2 GAPs (single sum, one structured set); they record Chang's `2/5` for GAPs of fixed rank and `1/3` for rank 2 as the prior state. Shkredov, *On the distribution of quadratic residues*, arXiv:2308.14069, Finite Fields Appl. 103 (2025) 102577, abstract: "We obtain the best upper bound on the number of gaps in ℜ at the moment and generalize this result for sets with small doubling". Neither touches the two-set bilinear sum.

### 1.2 Clique number (CITED)

Bandeira, Dmitriev, Lucca, Nizić-Nikolac, Rödder, *Randomstrasse101: Open Problems of 2025*, arXiv:2603.29571v1 (31 Mar 2026), and the blog post "On the clique number of the Paley Graph (problems 25–29)" (both fetched): Problem 25 "ω(G_p)=O(polylog(p))"; "Recently, Hanson and Petridis [HP (21)] showed that ω(G_p) ≤ (1+o(1))√(p/2)"; Problem 28 "There exists ε>0 such that the Sum-of-Squares relaxation of degree 4 for the clique number of the Paley graph gives a bound of O(p^{1/2−ε})", with "the authors prove that it cannot improve beyond Ω(p^{1/3})" (Kunisky–Yu); Problem 29 (Paley ETF RIP beyond the square-root bottleneck) open. No sub-`√p` bound is recorded. Kunisky's publication page (fetched) lists no Paley paper after arXiv:2303.16475 (Exp. Math. 34(4), 2025); OpenAlex lists zero 2024+ citers of Kunisky 2023 and of Kunisky–Yu.

Hanson–Petridis, local copy `sources/sigma-hanson-petridis-1905.09134.txt`, Theorem 1.2: "Let p be a prime and suppose A,B ⊆ F_p satisfy A+B ⊆ Z_d ∪ {0} for some d properly dividing p−1. Then |A||B| ≤ d + |B ∩ (−A)|." Corollary 1.5: "suppose A ⊆ F_p is such that A−A ⊆ Z_d ∪ {0}. Then |A|(|A|−1) ≤ d. In particular, for p ≡ 1 (mod 4), we have ω(G_p) ≤ (√(2p−1)+1)/2." And, verbatim, the field restriction: "The method developed to prove Theorem 1.2 only works in prime fields."

Yip, *Exact values and improved bounds on the clique number of cyclotomic graphs*, arXiv:2304.13213v5 (15 Aug 2025), Des. Codes Cryptogr. 93 (2025) 5131–5142: "the clique number of the Cayley graph Cay(F_q^+,S) is at most √|S/S| + √q/p"; new for generalized Paley graphs of non-square prime-power order; for `q=p`, `S=Q` it reads `√(p−1)+1`, weaker than HP. Assessment: no change to the prime-order bound.

Negative results on relaxations. Wang, Shen, Kobzar, *Lower Bounds on Block-Diagonal SDP Relaxations for the Clique Number of the Paley Graphs and Their Localizations* (2024; PDF fetched from people.math.osu.edu/kobzar.1/papers/paley.pdf, text extracted), Theorem 3.3: "For any level of hierarchy t ≥ 1, the value L^t(G_p) of the block-diagonal relaxation for the clique number of Paley graph G_p with p ≡ 1 (mod 4) satisfies L^t(G_p) ≥ √p/2^{t−1} + O(t/2^t)", and for the localization at a clique `K` of size `a`, "L^t(G_{p,K}) ≥ √p/2^{a+t−1} + O(a/2^t)"; by their Remark 3.4 this applies to the Lovász–Schrijver hierarchy. Gaar–Pucher, arXiv:2412.12958v2: "for Paley graphs, the bounds obtained from the ESH remain the Lovász theta function up to a certain threshold level" (computations `p ≤ 197`). Assessment: any fixed-level relaxation of these families is stuck at `c√p`; only a level growing with `p` could beat `√p`, which is the same conclusion as barrier B6 in `research/sigma-barriers-2026-09-05.md`.

### 1.3 Complete bicliques and Sárközy-type statements (CITED; the genuinely new results)

Kalmynin, *On additive irreducibility of multiplicative subgroups*, arXiv:2504.10202v2 (v1 14 Apr 2025, v2 28 May 2025; abstract and HTML fetched). Theorem 3: "For all primes p, the set R_p of quadratic residues modulo p is additively irreducible, i.e. R_p ≠ A+B with |A|,|B|>1." Theorem 2: "If μ_d = A+B with |A|,|B|>1, then |A|=|B|=√d." Theorem 1: "If μ_d is the set of all d-th roots of unity in F_p and A−A = μ_d ∪ {0} for some A ⊂ F_p then d=2 or 6." Method: "we employ a version of Stepanov's method, developed by Hanson and Petridis". Definitions used below: `HP(x;A,d) = Σ_{a∈A} c_a(A)(x+a)^{d+α−1} − 1` with `c_a(A) = 1/∏_{a'∈A∖{a}}(a−a')`; a pair is "d-critical if A+B ⊆ μ_d ∪ {0} and |A|·|B| = d + |(−A) ∩ B|"; Lemma 4: for a d-critical pair, "HP(x;A,d) = C ∏_{b∈B}(x−b)^{α−ε(b)}" with `ε(b)=1` iff `b ∈ B ∩ (−A)`. The HTML states no Paley clique or character-sum bound. Assessment: needs structure (the target set is a subgroup); it is a statement about complete bicliques, exactly the `S(A,B) = |A||B|` extreme, and gives no `p^{−δ}` cancellation.

Yip, Yoo, *Multiplicative subgroups are not restricted sumsets*, arXiv:2607.25711 (28 Jul 2026): "a proper multiplicative subgroup H ≤ F_p^* cannot satisfy H = A +̂ A whenever |H| ≥ 7", with decompositions only for `|H| ∈ {1,3,6}`. Yip, Yoo, *Additive decompositions of multiplicative subgroups in prime fields: a self-contained approach*, arXiv:2608.02568 (3 Aug 2026): self-contained reproof of the Kalmynin and Rudnev–Tyrrell classification, "the only nontrivial example is the subgroup of order 4". Rudnev, Tyrrell, *Multiplicative subgroups of prime fields are not sumsets*, arXiv:2607.24270 (v1 27 Jul 2026, v2 12 Aug 2026; abstract fetched): for a proper subgroup `H = A+B`, "either one of the summands is a singleton, or |A|=|B|=2 and |H|=4", by the Hanson–Petridis polynomial method building on Kalmynin (CITED; exactly the decomposition pattern found in T3, §4). Kim, Yip, Yoo, arXiv:2602.20919v2 (JLMS 114(3), 2026): `(G−1)∖{0}` is not a nontrivial product set and shifted cosets are not `A/A`; Kim, Yip, Yoo, arXiv:2309.09124v4 (Canad. J. Math.): "if a nontrivial shift of a multiplicative subgroup G contains a product set AB, then |A||B| is essentially bounded by |G|"; Yoo, arXiv:2607.24370 (27 Jul 2026): equality case. Cochrane, arXiv:2602.04111 (4 Feb 2026): a subgroup of `Z_p^*` is a GAP iff its size is 2, 4 or `p−1`; arXiv:2607.28559 (30 Jul 2026), sumsets and GAPs in subgroups. Assessment for all: subgroup-structured, complete-biclique statements; no cancellation exponent; the bicliques `A+B ⊆ Q` with `|A||B| ≤ p/2` are untouched, so nothing here is "sub-`√p`" in the sense of the brief.

## 3. The three most promising new techniques, and where each stops (Task 3)

**(A) The refined Hanson–Petridis polynomial (Kalmynin; Rudnev–Tyrrell; Yip–Yoo).** The technique is Stepanov's method with the auxiliary polynomial `HP(x;A,d)` of degree `d+α−1`, whose coefficients are chosen so that it vanishes to order `α` at every `b ∈ B` (order `α−1` if `−b ∈ A`); counting zeros gives `|A||B| ≤ d + |B∩(−A)|`, and the 2025 refinement (Lemma 4, verified exactly in §4 on 2400 critical pairs) is that equality forces `HP` to be exactly `C∏_b (x−b)^{α−ε(b)}`, whose coefficient identities are then exploited (Kalmynin's Lemma 13 congruence `2(3k−2)(k−1)α + (k+2)(k+3) ≡ 0 (mod p)`, quoted from the HTML, not re-derived here) to rule out equality for `Q_p`. It proves Sárközy's conjecture for every prime and the complete classification of additive decompositions of subgroups. It stops short of the conjecture for a structural reason: the zero count is at most the degree `d+α−1 < p`, and the coefficient step needs the binomial `C(d+α−1, α−1) ≢ 0 (mod p)`; these cap the conclusion at `|A||B| ≲ d`, i.e. `√(p/2)` in the balanced case, and give nothing for bicliques with `|A||B| ≤ p/2` and nothing at all for sums with cancellation (barrier B3). `F_{p²}` test: passed, and instructively so — HP write "The method developed to prove Theorem 1.2 only works in prime fields", and indeed over `F_{p²}` the subfield `F_p` is a clique of size `√q`, so the statement is false there; the prime-field fact used is the non-vanishing of binomial coefficients modulo `p` for degrees below `p`. That same fact is what pins the exponent at `1/2`, so the technique is prime-field-specific but not barrier-breaking.

**(B) Localization and SDP hierarchies (Kunisky; Wang–Shen–Kobzar; Gaar–Pucher).** The technique is to certify clique bounds by semidefinite relaxations of increasing level, or spectrally on localizations `G_{p,a}` (induced on common neighbours of an `a`-clique). What is proved in 2024–2025 is negative: `L^t(G_p) ≥ √p/2^{t−1}` and `L^t(G_{p,K}) ≥ √p/2^{a+t−1}` (Theorem 3.3), the ESH stays at `ϑ = √p` up to a threshold, and SOS-4 is `≥ Ω(p^{1/3})`. Hence every fixed level of these hierarchies, and every fixed localization depth, is stuck at a constant times `√p`; only level or depth growing with `log p` remains, which is exactly the workspace's growing-depth criterion (`research/sigma-pass-summary-2026-09-05.md` §3, `sigma-barriers` B6). `F_{p²}` test: failed in the sense that matters — the lower-bound constructions (Feige–Krauthgamer pseudomoments) use only the strongly-regular parameters of the Paley graph, which are the same over `F_q`, `q=p²`, where `ω = √q`; so these relaxations cannot distinguish the prime field at any fixed level, in agreement with B4.

**(C) The RIP–extractor–clique transfer (Satake 2405.08608; 2011.02907 Theorem 10).** The technique is to move between three formulations: `(K,δ)`-RIP of the Paley ETF, two-source extraction below the half barrier, and bilinear character sums, via the exact Gram identity `⟨φ_i,φ_j⟩ = (√−1)^r χ(a_i−a_j)/√p` (Satake 2011.02907, Lemma 12, local copy). What is proved is a set of conditional implications (Theorem 18, Corollary 31; Theorem 10 of 2011.02907 in the other direction), plus the elementary Lemma 28. It stops because each hypothesis is one of the open Problems 25/29 or Conjecture 7; the transfer is lossless but produces no exponent. `F_{p²}` test: the Gram identity and Lemma 28 (a Rayleigh-quotient inequality, `1ᵀM_U1 ≤ ‖M_U‖·|U|`) are field-agnostic and hold for the ETF built from `F_{p²}`, where the subfield clique gives `δ_{√q} ≥ (√q−1)/√q`; so "RIP beyond `√`" is itself a prime-field-only statement, and the transfer merely relabels the barrier.

A fourth line, Fouvry–Shparlinski–Xi's trilinear sums, is the closest to arbitrary sets (two of three variables arbitrary) but its prime-field-specific step is Burgess shifting along the interval variable, which is why the interval cannot be removed.
