# Quadruple orbits and an exact power factorization

**Status: the uniform energy estimate and both Paley targets remain open.**
This note proves a structural factorization for every dyadic order. It
isolates the contribution of four distinct elements to the fourth-energy
problem. The factorization supplies no adequate uniform bound on that
contribution. No novelty or Lean-formalization claim is made.

Use the polynomial from [kernel-discriminant.md](kernel-discriminant.md):

\[
 P_n(Y)=\prod_{j=1}^{n/2-1}\bigl(Y-(1+\zeta_n^j)^n\bigr),\qquad
 F_n=P_n(2^n),\quad \Delta_n=\operatorname{disc}(P_n),\quad A_n=\Delta_nF_n.
\]

All three integers are positive. Write `odd(a)=a/2^{v₂(a)}` and set
`T_n=odd(3^n−1)`. For n=2^k≥4, `T_n=(3^n−1)/2^{k+2}`: the case k=1
has 3²−1=8, and each doubling adds one factor of two since
`3^{2^k}+1≡2 mod 8`.

## The orbit identity

Work first in `Z/p^rZ` for an odd splitting prime p, with the order-n
subgroup H_r obtained by lifting the roots of unity. We will then allow
an unramified extension so the proof covers every odd prime.

Because −1 belongs to H_r, ordered solutions of `a+b=c+d` correspond
to ordered zero-sum quadruples. Those that can be partitioned into two
opposite pairs number `3n²−3n`: the three pairings each give n² solutions,
their three pairwise intersections each have n solutions, and their
triple intersection is empty since 2 is invertible.

Every remaining zero-sum multiset has one of three multiplicity types:

1. Three equal entries and one other entry. These are
   `{a,a,a,−3a}`. They exist exactly when `3∈H_r`, and then form one
   orbit of n multisets, each with four orderings.
2. One repeated entry and two distinct other entries. Write u_r for
   the number of these multiset orbits under multiplication by H_r.
   Every orbit has n multisets, each with twelve orderings. The action
   is free because any stabilizer must fix the unique repeated element.
3. Four distinct entries. Write v_r for their number of orbits. Their
   stabilizers are also trivial: every nontrivial subgroup of the dyadic
   group H_r contains −1, while a multiset fixed by −1 consists of two
   opposite pairs and was excluded. Each orbit contributes 24n orderings.

Four equal entries cannot sum to zero because 4 is invertible. Two equal
pairs have sum zero only when they are opposite pairs, already excluded.
Thus this classification is exhaustive. With `ε_r=1_{3∈H_r}`,

\[
 \boxed{E_2(H_r)=3n^2-3n+n(4\varepsilon_r+12u_r+24v_r).}
 \tag{1}
\]

In particular `D_r≡4ε_r mod 12`. This explains the fourth-energy excess
4 at some exceptional primes and the excesses divisible by twelve at
the others. It is more precise than divisibility of D_r by four alone.

## Separating the two repeated-entry contributions

Let `t_r=#{(b,c)∈H_r²:b+c=−2}`. Normalize the repeated element in a
multiset of type two to 1. Among these ordered pairs, `b=c=−1` is the
single opposite-pair case. When `3∈H_r`, the two pairs `(1,−3)` and
`(−3,1)` give the triple-entry case. All other pairs give type two,
twice each. Hence

\[
 t_r=1+2\varepsilon_r+2u_r.
 \tag{2}
\]

Let e_r denote the number of roots of P_n, with their original labels,
that equal 2^n modulo p^r. The kernel labeling argument gives
`t_r=1+2e_r`, so

\[
 e_r=\varepsilon_r+u_r.
 \tag{3}
\]

For completeness, one can obtain the labeling directly from `b+c=2`.
The ratio h=b/c is in H_r and `(1+h)^n=2^n`. Conversely such an h
gives the unique pair `c=2/(1+h)`, `b=hc`; the denominator is a unit
and the root condition puts c in H_r. The value h=1 gives one pair,
and every other inverse pair gives two. Negating b,c does not change
the count at −2.

## Valuations at every odd prime

For an arbitrary odd prime p, choose f with `n | p^f−1` and work in
the ring of integers O of an unramified extension of Q_p with residue
field F_(p^f). Here `v(p)=1`, so rational integers retain their ordinary
p-adic valuations. One construction is to lift a monic irreducible
degree-f polynomial over F_p; its residue extension is separable, and
the resulting complete local ring has maximal ideal generated by p.
The roots of `X^n−1` lift uniquely because their derivatives are units.

All arguments above apply in `O/p^r O`. Distinct roots of unity have
distinct residues, so `1+h` is a unit except when h=−1. The map on
units `x↦x^n` has precisely H_r as kernel, by the same simple-root
lifting argument. Therefore both the coset labeling and the additive
energy count remain valid. Reduction of the integer polynomial P_n is
justified by its identity modulo `Φ_n(X)=X^{n/2}+1`, as in the preceding
note. This use of extension residue rings is for the factorization of
an integer; it makes no assertion about the prime-field spectral target.

The boundary product and the discriminant identity now give

\[
 v_p(F_n)=\sum_{r\ge1}e_r,\qquad
 4v_p(A_n)=\sum_{r\ge1}D_r.
\]

Also `ε_r=1` precisely when `3^n≡1 mod p^r`; for p=3 both sides are
always false. Hence `Σ_r ε_r=v_p(3^n−1)`. Combining (1) and (3),

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 v_p(F_n)&=v_p(3^n-1)+\sum_r u_r,\\
 v_p(\Delta_n)&=2\sum_r u_r+6\sum_r v_r,\\
 v_p(A_n)&=v_p(3^n-1)+3\sum_r u_r+6\sum_r v_r.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{4}
\]

The sums are finite since F_n and Δ_n are nonzero. This proves the
following identities for positive odd integers U_n and V_n:

\[
 \boxed{\begin{aligned}
 \operatorname{odd}(F_n)&=T_n U_n,\\
 \operatorname{odd}(\Delta_n)&=U_n^2V_n^6,\\
 \operatorname{odd}(A_n)&=T_n U_n^3V_n^6.
 \end{aligned}}
 \tag{5}
\]

Moreover `v_p(U_n)=Σ_r u_r` and `v_p(V_n)=Σ_r v_r` for every odd p.
In particular the quotient `odd(A_n)/T_n` is the perfect cube
`(U_n V_n²)³`. This is proved uniformly in n, rather than inferred from
its small-order factorizations.

## What the identity leaves to prove

The contributions with repeated entries already cost only O(n²) energy.
Indeed t_r≤n, while (2) makes t_r odd, so
`u_r≤(n−2)/2−ε_r`. Formula (1) gives, for every eligible prime field,

\[
 3n^2-3n+24nv_1\le E_2(H)
 \le 9n^2-15n+24nv_1.
 \tag{6}
\]

Thus, in the quartic window, the required fourth-energy consequence
`E₂=O(n² log n)` is equivalent to `v₁=O(n log n)`. A bound
`v_p(V_n)=O(n log n)` would be sufficient but could be stronger, since
it counts collisions at all p-adic precisions. Neither bound is proved.

Factoring off a cube or a sixth power does not by itself improve the
order of the elementary height bound. For example (5) and the previous
bound on A_n only give

\[
 v_1\le v_p(V_n)\le\frac{\log_2 A_n}{6\log_2 p}
 \le\frac{(n+1)(n/2-1)^2}{6\log_2 p},
\]

which has order n³/log n in the working window and is worse than the
trivial order n² bound on the number of orbits. The exact structure
isolates a counting problem; it has not solved that problem. Even a
fourth-energy bound would still leave the high centered moments needed
for the desired spectral estimate.

## Exact checks

The script verifies the integer factorization without assuming a prime
factorization of its cube or sixth-power part:

| n | Bits in A_n | v₂(A_n) | Bits in U_n | Bits in V_n |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 4 | 5 | 2 | 1 | 1 |
| 8 | 62 | 27 | 9 | 1 |
| 16 | 582 | 176 | 70 | 30 |
| 32 | 4915 | 897 | 373 | 477 |
| 64 | 40003 | 4082 | 1730 | 5107 |

Here U_4=V_4=V_8=1 and U_8=459. The integer A_64 is computed by an
exact determinant, but is **not fully factored** and supplies no complete
order-64 exceptional-prime classification.

Direct pair enumeration, independent zero-sum multiset enumeration,
orbit normalization, polynomial products and lifted valuations agree
in 55 cases, covering 109 precision levels. These include every splitting
exception through n=32, the four larger examples in the preceding note,
and five non-splitting checks in unramified quadratic extension rings.
The extensions use `T²=d` with d explicitly verified to be a quadratic
nonresidue. A power lift `g↦g^{q^{r−1}} mod p^r` is independent of the
Taylor-correction lift used in the earlier script.

The two known quartic-window resonances have different orbit types:

| p | n | ε₁ | u₁ | v₁ | D₁ |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 6700417 | 64 | 0 | 1 | 0 | 12 |
| 67403009 | 128 | 0 | 0 | 1 | 24 |

At n=16,p=17 the first precision has `(ε,u,v)=(1,6,5)`, the second
has `(0,1,0)`, and the third has no nontrivial orbits. This independently
checks the higher-precision contribution, rather than assuming that
all valuations are determined at the first level.

Run `python3 experiments/quadruple_orbits.py`. Results, orbit representatives,
integer coefficients and hashes are in `results/quadruple_orbits.json`.
