The candidate tooling contribution is retaining information normally discarded after query interpolation: complete local supports when a candidate appears, and certified local agreement caps where it does not. These are elementary consequences of querying every basis subset in an arc block. They produce explicit, independently checked pruning traces and large coordinate-evaluation reductions on the measured inputs. The late-rejection control prevents promoting those observations into a uniform complexity result.

Queries included “list decoding pruning candidate verification interpolation subsets frequency agreement polynomial deterministic”, “Reed Solomon list decoding pruning early termination candidate verification agreement count”, and “list decoding binomial candidate pruning”. Search results establish overlaps, not historical absence. No claim that this exact interface has never been tried is supported. A complete implementation-level prior-art comparison remains open.
