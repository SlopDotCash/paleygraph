# Independent observable and histogram-trade review

Reviewed on 2026-09-05: `observables/fiber_audit.py`, `observables/moment_trades.py`, all three saved fiber suites, and the saved trade report. This review did not modify the implementations.

The implementation correctly distinguishes target non-determination from failure of a uniform upper bound. It retains the Gaussian sixth-moment threshold as a diagnostic baseline and explicitly excludes the sixth squarefree aggregate as an independent predictor because the reconstruction identity makes it target-equivalent after lower and boundary terms are supplied.

The independent standard-library verifier [replay_observable_certificates.py](replay_observable_certificates.py) imports no discovery code, NumPy, or SymPy. It constructs quadratic characters by residue membership, replays all 60 saved witness endpoints, and checks moment, boundary, additive-energy and quartic-deck equality from the original sets. All pass. Its saved evidence is [observable_review.json](observable_review.json), including hashes of reviewed result files.

The primitive move `(-10,15,-6,1)` on absolute interior levels `(0,2,4,6)` preserves moments 0,2,4 and changes the sixth moment by 23040. A nonzero 3-by-3 Vandermonde minor is 768, giving one-dimensional rational kernel; the last move coordinate is 1, so every integral kernel vector is an integral multiple. The boundary squared levels `(1,9,25)` have Vandermonde determinant 3072, so size and boundary moments 2,4 determine the three boundary masses. These facts independently confirm the mathematical explanation in the trade prototype. They are elementary integer-kernel mathematics, with Markov-basis methods as prior art (L18).

An independent exhaustive enumeration of all 17,550 normalized six-sets over F29 reproduces the trade census: 64 actual feature fibers, 2 with a strict gap between the actual maximum and the nonnegative histogram-relaxation maximum, and largest gap 23040. The independent relaxation uses brute force over feasible integer moves, not the prototype's interval formula. Full F41 and F61 enumerations were reviewed at algorithm level but not independently re-enumerated in this lane.

The F61 example preserving all admitted features, including the entire unordered quartic deck, is a real prime-field finite certificate. It does not show that labeled quartic incidence data fail, and the unordered-deck choice should remain explicit. The F61 and F257 six-set collisions are also outside the critical slice `p >= n^4`; that slice starts at p >=1296 for n=6. Results on the critical-scale holdouts should therefore be reported separately. Sparse sampled fibers can look unambiguous simply because almost every feature vector is unique; lack of collisions in a sample is not sufficiency.

The supplied suites use safe small integer ranges. General reuse needs input validation: reject sample counts exceeding the finite population (otherwise the sampler can loop forever); restrict or correctly implement n<4 (the current quartic array is empty); and enforce a conservative integer-overflow bound before scaling NumPy int64 moment calculations. These review comments were sent to the root implementer for resolution. They do not invalidate the checked configured runs.

The finite novelty claim remains a specific implementation and witness family, not invention of moment fibers, integer-kernel moves, or separating observables. The useful new research question is which inexpensive arithmetic constraints remove the unrealizable histogram extrema without simply encoding M6 itself.
