# Tooling novelty audit — 2026-09-05

This audit concerns tools for discovering missing information. It attempts no prize proof. It reviewed the current Paley frontier and selected Proximity Prize source and failure records, then searched primary literature. The search is bounded; it cannot certify that an idea has never been conceived, privately tried, or published under different terminology.

The strongest defensible novelty standard is: **a precise proposed combination, checked against the cited literature and local record, with a new executable diagnostic or finite certificate.** None of the five proposals below has established global novelty. A new name, new implementation, or new dataset is not by itself a new mathematical method.

## Five precise proposals

### 1. Exact target-separating observables on actual arithmetic inputs

**Input:** a finite family X of genuine character kernels or polynomial-code configurations; a declared target T; admissible observables f1,...,fr; an explicit symmetry action preserving T.

**Operation:** partition X by its observable vector. For each fiber, compute the full target range and record a pair attaining distinct target values. Rank a new observable by the reduction of target ambiguity per evaluation cost. Mark an observable as `target_equivalent`, `target_dependent`, or `independent_candidate` before using its score. Reserve unseen primes, sizes, and structural families for transfer tests.

**Finite certificate:** x,y with f(x)=f(y), T(x)≠T(y), checked from the original field data by a small verifier. This certifies non-determination by f. It does **not** disprove every upper bound in f: the fiber maximum is itself an upper bound. To reject a proposed inequality, the target must exceed its explicitly declared budget.

**Prototype ladder:** enumerate normalized small subsets containing 0 and 1; compare M2,M4 and additive energy against M6; add corrected quartic information and record whether it simply encodes M6. Next use field-preserving mutations to search within fibers at larger p. Full-problem relevance would require a uniform inequality involving the surviving observable, not just finite predictive performance.

**Closest known ideas:** separating invariants, truncated moment problems, CEGAR, feature-guided mathematical discovery [L1–L4, L15]. The potential contribution is the target-relative arithmetic restriction, explicit leakage control, and portable finite witness format. The general separation/refinement principle is known.

### 2. A constraint-preserving adversary and arithmetic-realization checker

**Input:** a list of constraints with provenance and an objective, such as maximize a restricted spectral outlier. Store which constraints are exact polynomial equations, inequalities, floating tolerances, or field-realizability requirements.

**Operation:** optimize within the current relaxation; reduce each adversarial witness to a small support; find a low-cost actual arithmetic identity it violates; add the identity and repeat. Maintain two disjoint populations: actual prime-field inputs and synthetic relaxed models. Passing every listed test never promotes a synthetic model to an actual character table.

**Prototype ladder:** reproduce an existing moment-matching model, add exact Gram/projection identities, then translation/multiplicativity constraints; compare with prime-field and square-field examples. Export either an exact witness or a proof certificate for an optimization bound. Failure to find a witness is not infeasibility.

**Closest known ideas:** CEGAR, SOS pseudoexpectations and pseudocalibration [L1, L5]. Paley degree-four SOS has an explicit lower-bound barrier [L6]. New work would be in the realizability interface and automatic selection of arithmetic constraints, not in adversarial optimization itself.

### 3. A signed contraction compiler with explicit boundary terms

**Input:** a signed sum of character kernels, retaining anchors, repeated-variable patterns, coefficients and exceptional zeros. Represent contractions by colored graphs with labeled input/output vertices.

**Operation:** canonicalize graph shapes; identify exact collision partitions; cancel before taking absolute values; compute connected or cumulant coordinates by a declared partition transform; retain every removed diagonal and boundary term in a reconstruction identity. Rank shapes by actual signed contribution, not their termwise absolute sum. Try invariant-basis orthogonalization only relative to a specified reference measure and retain change-of-basis conditioning.

**Prototype ladder:** reconstruct the exact fourth and sixth moments on small prime fields from graph records; recover existing obstruction witnesses; then test mixed words and all symmetry sectors at increasing depth. The measurable question is whether compression preserves cancellation and has stable rank/conditioning. The full-problem obligation is a uniform estimate at the necessary growing depth for the remaining connected aggregate.

**Closest known ideas:** traffic probability, graph matrices, tensor cumulants, and finite tensor moment-cumulant transforms [L7–L10]. Recent tensor cumulants are especially close: they supply invariant polynomial bases, so “invent tensor cumulants” would be false. Existing local cumulant programs also prevent a novelty claim for subtraction of Wick terms. A possible new tool would be a deterministic finite-field, anchor-aware compiler with exact arithmetic correction records. No theorem transfers from random/unitarily invariant tensors merely by changing names.

### 4. A degree-and-specialization atlas of polynomial-code dependencies

**Input:** evaluation points, labeled agreement regions, locator polynomials, and cofactor degree budgets. Construct exact coefficient matrices of the allowed syzygies for each budget.

**Operation:** compute rank, kernel bases, minimal support dependencies, and nonzero-minor certificates over the actual field; compare with symbolic generic rank for the same incidence pattern. Track which dependencies persist under deleting roots or modifying agreement regions, and which exist only in one characteristic. Keep rank deficiency distinct from realizability of a received-word stack meeting the prize budgets.

**Prototype ladder:** replay the F41/μ20 sextic configuration from SYZ71; enumerate smaller disjoint locator triples and perturb roots; compare random evaluation sets, subgroups, and extensions. Use modular rank and sparse elimination at larger sizes, followed by exact witness replay. The full-problem insight would be an identifiable arithmetic family of rank drops with a uniform exclusion/count theorem and a verified stack-lift connection.

**Closest known ideas:** μ-bases, matroid circuits, higher-order MDS and GM-MDS [L11–L13]. All are already recognized in the local syzygy lane. The potential contribution is a common certificate format linking degree, specialization, and realizability failures; an ordinary rank table is useful engineering, not a new mathematical theory. Generic RS results do not automatically cover fixed roots-of-unity specializations.

### 5. An observability audit for symmetry-reduced spectral methods

**Input:** the full restricted operator, its finite symmetry group, and chosen probes/seeds.

**Operation:** construct every symmetry sector; measure the rank of the probe-generated spaces in each sector; report the complement, off-block residual, border coupling and extremal Rayleigh witnesses. Add a seed only if it supplies previously absent information. Compare exact rational Rayleigh certificates with numerical eigenvalues, distinguishing a lower witness from an upper certificate.

**Prototype ladder:** reproduce the existing S3 decomposition and the invisible complement; compare uniform, orbit, and coordinate seeds; replay prime-field and square-field outliers. Scale by matrix-vector products with traceable field arithmetic rather than allocating all tensor powers. A full-problem result still needs a quantitative bound on the unobserved or observed extremal sector; coverage alone does not prove an edge.

**Closest known ideas:** symmetry reduction of SDP/SOS [L14], Krylov methods, and the existing Paley spectral route [L16]. The potential contribution is automated completeness and failure certificates tailored to the actual arithmetic operator. Decomposition and block iteration are known methods and were already used locally.

## Research policy for iteration

Every candidate should carry: its exact domain and quantifiers; the closest prior art; its structural invariant; a finite evaluator; a negative control; a replayable certificate; the missing theorem required for scale; and the conditions that would retire it. Store failed approaches with the same care as successful examples.

A method earns continuation when it supplies a new low-complexity separator, a new exact structural identity, or an experimentally stable compression that survives structural holdouts. It does not earn continuation merely by getting a larger numerical example, renaming an open estimate, or matching a fixed number of moments.

Finite separation, algorithmic novelty, theorem novelty, and priority are separate claims. Global historical novelty is **not established** for any proposal here. The primary-source ledger and literal query log are in [literature_ledger.md](literature_ledger.md).
