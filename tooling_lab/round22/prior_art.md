# Scope of the novelty claim

No worldwide-first claim is established. The proposed contribution is the exact arithmetic interface between this cyclotomic digit code, its scalar quotient, known-coordinate equations, and independently reviewable finite decoder bounds. Its ingredients have substantial prior art.

- **Norm approximation and dual certificates.** Boyd and Vandenberghe's [duality slides](https://web.stanford.edu/class/ee364a/lectures/duality.pdf), slide5.29 (PDF page31), give the dual of norm approximation: a linear functional annihilating the approximation space, constrained in the dual norm. Here the primal is min_lambda ||q-M_K^T lambda||1; a certificate eta obeys M_K eta=0 and ||eta||infinity<=1. Exact primal/dual equality proves optimality. Slides5.3 and5.35 also give the lower-bound principle and Farkas alternatives. These are established facts; the application and saved arithmetic certificates are this experiment's work. The cited sections were inspected directly.
- **LP discovery.** The installed SciPy1.15.3 [HiGHS interface](https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy-1.15.3/reference/optimize.linprog-highs.html) documents equality/inequality constraints, variable bounds and solver status. The prototype explicitly permits free lambda variables and normalizes q before discovery. Floating solver success is neither an accepted proof nor an optimality certificate; rational identities and feasible dual vectors are checked separately.
- **Lattice coordinates.** The [fpylll API](https://fpylll.readthedocs.io/en/master/modules.html) exposes integer matrices and LLL basis transformations. Changing the Euclidean metric by applying an exact linear embedding, then checking the resulting unimodular transform, is an established lattice technique. This prototype uses adjugate images of erased digit vectors as that embedding. No claim is made that LLL finds an optimal basis.
- **Joint convex geometry.** Dadush, Peikert and Vempala's [Enumerative Lattice Algorithms in Any Norm via M-Ellipsoid Coverings](https://arxiv.org/abs/1011.5666) studies lattice enumeration in convex bodies and applications to shortest/closest vectors and integer programming. Its abstract was inspected; its general algorithms are not implemented here. Searching lattice points in a projected convex body, or separating them with linear functionals, is not a new general mathematical idea.

The distinction to retain is testable: an individual radius can be optimal while the Cartesian product of all coordinate intervals remains very loose. Exact coupled cuts investigate that loss on this particular code. A rational witness in a continuous relaxation is not a pair of actual codewords. None of these certificates bounds the norm aggregates needed by the Paley investigation or discovers ambient Reed–Solomon clusters for the proximity problem.

Searches were conducted on September6,2026, with primary sources prioritized. Negative search results cannot prove an idea has never been tried, including in unpublished work or under other terminology.
